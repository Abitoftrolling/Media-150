Reading and Written Response #1

“Graphic Novel: Motherhood in crisis” by Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah reflection

Since this is a media/design course, I should at least mention some parts of the background that interest me. The pulsing red in the background that slowly grows into a full red background as the title of the op-ed slowly reveals itself as you scroll down. I, however, wish that the transition from the red/reddish black into a full white page was done better. That is, as all things i say for the most part, just a nitpick. After a short review on what the nuclear war is and its main factors for the start of it, we then return to the red pulse with the caption Imagine, A Nuclear Weapon is Launched and this is finally where things get a bit crazy. As you scroll down, a nuclear warhead fades in and into the black background slowly getting smaller and smaller until BOOM a color of bright orange, yellow, light red and black swirling in perfect harmony as we get closer to the eye of the typhoon then it [again] fades to white…joining it comes a roaring fire, concealing the aftermath of the bomb. Cars flying, houses reduced to ash. Although not seen in the rendition of the explosion, the author mentions that even the small animals in the area were reduced to flaming piles of flesh and bone. We then get another artist drawing of the aftermath; in this one you can see all the cars and debris piling on top of each other as the picture zooms in closer and closer to a car with 3 skeletons, 2 seeming to be children. Fire then quickly fades in and out as we go to the next scene with trees, houses and a helicopter surrounding the burning ground. It then cuts to a horrifying view of a mushroom cloud, rotten and black from the “asphalt, steel, soil, glass, flesh and bone.” The remains of those things slowly float towards the ground. The scene sits on this imagery for but a moment, as the sky grows into a violent red and the image brings us closer and closer to the cloud…then it fades to black; hints of red and yellow from the ensuing bomb giving a reminder to others. After a short interlude of details regarding a potential use of a nuclear weapon in Moscow, we get another thought to shudder at: “Imagine the damage the weapon would wreak on people and the environment.” We get yet another depiction of the aftermath caused by nuclear weapons depicting crops and farm vehicles being ravaged by “black rain”: contaminated water that had been picked up by the clouds and clouds start precipitating. The rain erodes the soil making agriculture impossible post nuclear fallout. These effects last for years, if not centuries before going back to the norm and even then, is it really the same soil that you were used to so long ago? With this we then get one last graphic starting with some food for thought: “Imagine the ripple effect of one nuclear warhead on the world — on where people live, what they eat, their sense of safety.” We then get a seemingly normal look of the earth’s surface before suddenly lines fade in, signalling missiles being sent to cities around the world, which could cause massive loss of life and food, which then would cause mass casualties due to starvation. This article is a great presentation of what would happen during and post nuclear fallout and how close this reality really is.
